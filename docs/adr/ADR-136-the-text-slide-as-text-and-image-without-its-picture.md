# ADR-136 — The text slide as the text-and-image slide without its picture

Status: Implemented on 2026-11-06 in the working tree; not committed, not published. Takes the place of the body and the card of ADR-094's text slide, and of the part of ADR-135 that kept them.

## Request

After ADR-135 was published: "זה נראה כאילו ה-layout של text לא השתנה (הוא לא דומה במבנה ל-טקסט+תמונה)". Asked which structure, with three drawn choices, the user chose "Text+image without the picture": the text-and-image slide's placeholders without the picture (kicker, title, three headed points, footer), its column across the width, with its numbers and rules.

## Context

- ADR-135 seated the kicker and the footer on the text slide, and kept what ADR-094 drew: one body from margin to margin, about 1600px wide, on a card or panel. The text-and-image slide is a column beside a picture: the kicker, the title, three points (a heading over its body, numbered, often on a thread or between rules) and the footer. The two looked like different families, and a slide moved from text and image to text lost its points' structure (three headings and three bodies have one body to go to).

## Decision

- **The contract.** `text` seats `caption, title, subtitle ×3, body ×3, footer`: textImage's without the image (`builtin/contract.ts`; the agent's guide in `packages/prompts/src/template.ts`, which says the text layout is textImage's without the image).
- **The layout.** The text slide keeps its head (the kicker, the title across the slide on one line) and its foot (footer, logo, slide number) as ADR-135 drew them, and the ground the template's slides share. Between them, the text-and-image slide's points: the same placeholders in the same text styles, at the same height, and what that slide draws with them (numbers, threads, rules between them, a card for each point, a panel behind them), moved from the column beside the picture to the width the column's outer margin leaves on both sides. What spans the column spans the slide; what is narrow keeps its place by its side. Nothing of the picture or its frame, and nothing of the old card and the ornaments drawn beside it. Changing a slide from one layout to the other moves nothing but the picture and the width.
- **One way of doing it.** `pointsAcross(layout)` (`packages/templates/src/pointsAcross.ts`) takes the points of a text-and-image layout across the slide. `withStandardLayouts` uses it for a template without a text layout of its own (under the title, where the content slides put theirs); a template whose text-and-image slide has a single body gets that body across the width, and one without a text-and-image slide three plain points.
- **The authored templates** (`authoring/<id>.ts`, 14): the points of the text-and-image layout are `textPoints(name, width)`, which both layouts call, the text layout with the width to the end margin (`WIDTH - offset`). Rules and rows drawn to the points' width (Tavlin's seeds, Taarucha's rules) are drawn to it; what is drawn upright in the other direction (Gir's chalk figures) stays so. The drawings only the old cards used are gone.
- **The library's templates** (21 that exist only as the catalog) were rewritten from their own text-and-image layout through `pointsAcross`: the old text layout's head, foot and the ground it shares with the text-and-image slide kept; the dashed and dotted rules drawn again to the new width (Shvil, Hod, Sirtut, Luach) rather than stretched; Zohar's soft glow left behind so that its panel meets the margin. Zerem's hand-drawn mirror of the old text slide is gone: the new one mirrors as its text-and-image slide does.
- **The samples**, in every language (sixteen each): the first three of the four points of the old text slide, each its bold opening as the heading (the colon taken off) and the rest as the body (as a sentence, capitalised where the language has case); the lead and the fourth point are gone. Titles that counted four points now count three, in every language (Shvil, Migdal, Nof, Defus, Ariach, Hod, Mifgash, Rakia, and Shvav's Chinese one). The authored templates hold them in their copy as `items: [title, body][]` (Hadran's `setlist`; `technology/<lang>.ts` `points`), as their text-and-image slides do.
- **The rule.** `AGENTS.md` says it of every template; `textSlideGaps` in `authoring/check.ts` holds the text layout to the text-and-image one: the same points in the same styles and order, the bodies at least 300px wider, a heading no narrower, nothing meeting that does not meet there, nothing in the foot; and the sample fills the kicker, the footer and the three points.

## Verification

- Unit tests: `packages/prompts` (the deck prompt within its budget, the layouts sentence shortened for it); `standardLayouts.test.ts` (the points of Tzuk's text-and-image slide across the width under the table's title, with their numbers and rules); `technology/samples.test.ts`. `tsc` of the package and of the authoring folder; ESLint of the changed files (the errors it reports in the authoring folder are in code this change did not touch).
- `check.ts` of the 14 authored templates: ok.
- The staged catalog (35 templates): every template valid, the contract kept, `textSlideGaps` clean, every sample drawn in all sixteen languages (what the validator still lists is the editability of other layouts, as published). A slide changed from text to text and image and back, and the other way, keeps the words of its kicker, title, headings, bodies and footer in every template, Hebrew and English. The round trip of `builtin.test.ts` over it: 2,380 of 2,380.
- `desktop/src/templates/services.test.ts`: a template the agent drafts without a text-and-image layout gets a text layout of three plain points (`title, subtitle ×3, body ×3`); the two expectations say so. The browser tests of the desktop were not run: they read the published library.
- Rendered at 640px against the staged catalog: the text slide beside the text-and-image slide of all 35 in Hebrew, and in English; the text slide of all 35 in all sixteen languages (560), with the review page's measure and the design check, looked at in Hebrew, English, Arabic, Japanese, German and Hindi.
- What the measure reports beyond the titles' outY of every slide: the headings of Shvil, Bolet and Maslul in every language, Defus's in Russian and Sirtut's in Russian and Vietnamese, 5–12px of ink over their box, which the same headings report on the text-and-image slide.
- Design check: the near-alignments (L09) on the text slides are those of the same template's text-and-image slide. The word count (L13), over 60 on the old text slide in nearly every template and language (35 of 35 in Hebrew and in English), is over it now in Vietnamese (30, counted by syllable), Spanish (11), French (10), Portuguese (8), Hindi (7), Italian (4) and Russian (1). The empty band under the content (L17) is gone where the points stood higher (they stand where the text-and-image slide has them), but on Dofek, Hadran and Tefer it is 5–8px over the limit, the bodies taking one line across the slide. On Lavan the head's rule and the three rules of the points stand in one column with unequal gaps (L10).
