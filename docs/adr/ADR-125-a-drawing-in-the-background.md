# ADR-125 — A drawing in the background

Status: Implemented on 2026-11-05 in the working tree, as the first of the stages below; not committed at the time of this record. It amends [ADR-094](ADR-094-layout-objects.md) for one kind of drawing: a template's drawing may belong to the background, not to the slide. It is the base for a collection of backgrounds in Elements, including animated ones, which is not built yet.

## Request

The user looked at a layout of a dark template with rows of coloured bars down one side, which have been objects of the slide since ADR-094, and asked why they are not part of the layout's background. Then:

"הכוונה לא להשאיר בכל ה-layout. אלא שב-layout שזה קיים - להפוך לרקע. כלומר, עוד תמונת רקע. זה כמובן דרישה תשתיתית רחבה יותר למקרים נוספים בתבנית ובתבניות אחרות." "אפשר יהיה להעביר ידנית לרקע אחר מהתבנית. זה מספיק טוב."

And before the work began: "שים לב שבהמשך ארצה ש"רקעים" יהיו קבוצה באלמנטים, כולל אפשרות שתהיה בהם אנימציה."

## Context

- A background (`Background`) had a fill and an overlay, plus blur and dimming for photos. It had no place for a drawing. Everything a layout draws belonged either to the slide (`objects`: each slide holds copies) or to the layout (`decorations`: drawn under every slide of the layout, and no slide can drop them).
- The user wants the bars to be neither. The layout draws them, they stay out of the way of the user's work, and they go when the slide takes another background in the Background tool. That tool already offers the template's background, the theme's variants, and a fill of the slide's own.
- The first plan was a drawing of the layout alone (`Layout.ground`). It would serve this case but not the next. A background chosen from Elements is a background, put on one slide or on all of them, so it needs the drawing inside the background itself. The first plan would have meant two places for one thing, and a migration between them later.

## Decision

- **The model.** `Background.elements` is optional: elements at their places on the slide, bottom to top. They are drawn over the fill and the overlay, and under the layout's decorations and the slide's elements. Any background can have one: the theme's, its variants, a layout's, a slide's. `Background` moved from `schema/primitives.ts` to `schema/background.ts`, since it now holds elements, and `Alpha` is now exported for it. A deck without drawings is unchanged, and no migration is needed.
- **What goes with the background.** A slide shows its own background, else its layout's, else the theme's, as before, and the drawing belongs to whichever one it shows. A slide that picks a variant or a fill of its own drops the layout's drawing; choosing the template's background again brings it back. Changing the fill in the Background tool keeps the drawing, because `withFill` keeps every field it does not change. The tool has a new action, **Remove the background drawing**, shown when the background the slide shows has a drawing. It gives the slide that background without the drawing (`withoutDrawing`, `objects/background.ts`), in all sixteen languages of the interface. **Apply to all slides** spreads the background as the slide shows it, drawing included.
- **Drawing it.** `SlideRenderer` draws the elements right after `BackgroundLayers`, the same way it draws decorations (`data-decoration-id`, no slot). So nothing on the Stage selects them and the runtime does not animate them. Everything drawn through the renderer shows them: the Background tool's swatches and those of the Templates panel, the New slide picker, and the thumbnails. Their React keys are kept apart from the decorations' keys, since the two lists may use the same ids.
- **Turning the direction.** `mirrorBackground` now mirrors the drawing along with the gradients, so a layout's or a slide's drawing moves to the other side. The theme's backgrounds keep their fills unmirrored, as always, but their drawings now change sides too. `changeDirection` does this with `mirrorBackgroundDrawing`, and adds a `theme.update` only when one of them has a drawing. `flipBackgrounds` makes a background drawn by hand for the other direction; when that background has no drawing of its own, it keeps the mirrored one.
- **Switching template.** The drawing in a layout's or a slide's background follows the new theme the way a layout's decorations do: the colours inside its CSS fills, its corners and its shadow (`followTheme`).
- **The design check.** The drawing of the background the slide shows is counted with the layout's decorations, as part of what is drawn under the slide (`SlideContext.layout`). A slide over the bars is not text alone (L16). The drawing stops counting when the slide takes another background.
- **PowerPoint.** The drawing becomes shapes: on the layout for a layout's background, on the master for the theme's, on the slide for the slide's. PowerPoint draws a layout's and a master's shapes on every slide that uses them. So a slide with a background of its own, whose inherited background has a drawing, hides those shapes (`showMasterSp="0"`) and draws the layout's decorations itself, as a slide with its own footer already did. A layout with a background of its own hides the master's drawing. The shapes of a drawing stand in no placeholder and take no part in the slide's animations.
- **The agent.** It sees the drawing when it reads a slide or the theme, but it writes backgrounds without one. `slide_update`, `theme_update` and `template_create` take `AgentBackground`, which is `Background` without `elements` (`agent-tools/src/tools/shared.ts`). With the elements included, those three tool definitions would each have copied the whole element schema, and the listing grew from under 99 KB to 153 KB. A background the agent writes replaces the old one whole, drawing included.

## Stages

1. **This record:** the drawing in the model, the renderer, the Background tool, direction, template switching, the design check, PowerPoint and the agent.
2. **The library's templates:** move the drawings that are atmosphere of a layout, not its content, into the layout's background, one layout at a time and chosen by eye. A geometric rule cannot tell atmosphere from content. Most templates exist only as the library's catalog ([ADR-114](ADR-114-no-copy-of-the-library.md)). For those, this is a change to `templates/catalog.json`, sent through Slidr Library's API: the chosen objects of a layout move from `objects` to `background.elements`. The templates authored in `packages/templates/src/authoring` move them in their source. `withObjects`, `lockedDrawings` and `authoring/check.ts` need no change: a drawing in the background is not one of the layout's decorations. `AGENTS.md` says when a drawing belongs to the background. Zerem is the first ([below](#addition-zerems-stream-2026-11-05)).
3. **Backgrounds in Elements:** a collection of backgrounds, each a `Background` with its drawing, that a slide or the whole deck takes.
4. **Backgrounds that move:** an animation that plays in a loop, without a step of the slide's timeline. The model has no such animation yet. The elements of a drawing have ids, so an animation can name them.

## Consequences

- Nothing in the library has a drawing in its background yet. Every template still gives its bars, cards and lines as objects (ADR-094) until stage 2.
- The slides of existing decks keep the copies they hold as objects. How a deck takes a template whose drawing has moved into the background is for stage 2 to settle.
- The PowerPoint file holds a drawing as shapes the user can select there, as it holds decorations.
- The studio's copy of the app's packages (`vendor/`) takes this with its next update. Its measure of how much of a slide is the slide's own ("N% editable") has to count a background's drawing as background, not as a drawing the layout keeps. That has not been checked.

## Left

- Find and replace reads the text of a layout's decorations, but not text in a background's drawing.
- HTML import makes no drawings in backgrounds.
- `flipsFrom` (`desktop/src/templates/actions.ts`) has a case where a drawing can be lost. When a personal template is saved from a deck whose layout the deck changed, the layout takes the background the source template drew by hand for the other direction. A drawing in the deck's own copy of that background is not carried over. No action in the app changes a layout's drawing today.

## Checks

- **Unit tests:**
  - Renderer: the order (fill, drawing, decorations); the drawing is gone under a slide's own background; the theme's drawing shows on a layout without a background.
  - Templates: `mirrorBackground` and `mirrorBackgroundDrawing`. `changeDirection` turns every drawing, the theme's included, leaves the theme's fills, is one undo step, and turning back gives the deck back. `followTheme` follows in a slide's drawing and in a kept layout's.
  - Design check: L16 counts the drawing only while it is shown.
  - PowerPoint: the layout's drawing is on the layout under its decorations. A slide with its own background hides the layout's shapes and draws its decorations. The master draws the theme's drawing, and a layout with its own background hides it.
  - Desktop: `withFill` keeps the drawing, and `withoutDrawing` removes it.
  - The agent's tool listing stays under 99 KB, and the translation test passes with the new string.
- **Runs:** the tests of `model`, `renderer`, `lint`, `pptx` and `agent-tools` pass, as do `desktop/src/objects/background.test.ts` and the i18n tests. All of `templates` passes except one test: a deck going to another template and back, for every pair of templates. That test times out at its limit of 120 seconds, and it does the same without this change (below). Type-checking passes for every package. In `desktop`, the only error is in `packages/runtime/src/presets.ts`, from a change made in parallel in the same working tree (the `disable` and `enable` animations). That change also accounts for two failures that appeared during the work. The size of `element_add` went from 30,984 to 31,059 characters, over its limit of 31,000, when `disabled` was added to every element; this record does not touch the element schema. - **Without this change:** two tests were also run on the last commit, in a worktree of it: the round trip of every pair of templates (166 s there, 170 s here, against the 120 s limit) and `desktop/src/ai/look.test.ts`. - **Not seen in the window:** nothing in the library has a drawing yet. The new action of the Background tool and a drawing on the Stage were checked by the tests above, not by eye.

## Addition: Zerem's stream (2026-11-05)

- **Request.** Asked to confirm which template the picture showed, the user approved ("מאשר"), then corrected: "זה היה זרקם ולא קוד", and asked to finish and publish: "להשלים ולפרסם". The first guess, Kod, was wrong. The placeholders in the picture are those of Zerem's opening layout exactly (the title at 644, 310, 1180 × 286; the subtitle at 784, 634, 1040 × 160), and the rows of bars are its stream.
- **What moved.** The stream is one group in three layouts of Zerem: the opening (`o_zerem_hero_stream`, 30 lanes), the text slide (`o_zerem_text_stream`, the dim strip of 7 lanes under the text panel) and the closing (`o_zerem_closing_stream`, 20 lanes). In each, in the layouts of both directions, it left the layout's objects for the drawing of the layout's background, which already had a CSS fill of its own. The three copies the template's sample held went with it. In each layout the stream is the lowest object and lies under no decoration, so the picture does not change. Every other byte of the catalog is as it was: the other templates, the samples in every language, the pictures, and `originals`, which only tests read.
- **How.** `toBackground` (`builtin/kit.ts`) moves objects of a template's layouts into their backgrounds, for a template authored here and for one that exists only as the catalog. It refuses a move that would change the picture: a drawing over a decoration or over an object under it, an id no layout has, or a copy on a sample slide that was changed or is animated. `authoring/tools/to-background.ts` runs it on a catalog file. The library writes nothing from the catalog in a publication of files, so `templates/zerem/template.json` goes with it, written as `templateFiles` (`desktop/build/media.ts`) writes it. Written from the catalog before the change, both files came out byte for byte as the library had them.
- **Checked.** The tests of `toBackground` (`builtin/withObjects.test.ts`). Every slide of Zerem's sample in Hebrew (17) and English (16) was rendered on the review page at 1280 px twice: by a dev server reading the published library, and by one reading a stand-in library with the new catalog. Each server had its own caches, and Chrome drew both. All 33 pairs are identical to the pixel. In the DOM of the second, the stream is drawn as the background's (`data-decoration-id`) on the opening, text and closing slides in both languages, and no slide element draws it.
- **Not yet published.** The catalog and `template.json` were not sent: this machine has no token of the publish scope (`LIBRARY_TOKEN`). `publish.mjs`, kept outside the repository, makes the publication (`PUT /api/library/staged/<sha256>` for each file, then `POST /api/library/files` with the hash each was made from). It sends nothing when the library changed either file since. The catalog goes from `abf5a3ad…` to `7ffccc1a…`, and `templates/zerem/template.json` from `eb1d07a4…` to `792a262d…`.
- **What publishing does to the apps already installed.** An app reads each built-in template with `Template.safeParse` (`desktop/src/templates/builtIn.ts`), and the schema rejects a field it does not know. Every installed version predates this record, so each one leaves Zerem out of its templates until it takes a version that has it. The studio, whose copy of the app's packages (`vendor/`) is older, cannot read Zerem either until that copy is updated. The user asked to publish knowing this. To undo it, publish the two files as they were, from `7ffccc1a…` and `792a262d…` back to `abf5a3ad…` and `eb1d07a4…`.
- **Left.** Decks made on Zerem before keep the stream as an object of their slides. It is not checked what a template applied again does with those copies.
