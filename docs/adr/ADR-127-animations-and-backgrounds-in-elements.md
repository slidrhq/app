# ADR-127 — Animations and backgrounds in Elements

Status: Implemented on 2026-11-05 in the working tree; **not committed** at the time of this record. It builds stages 3 and 4 of [ADR-125](ADR-125-a-drawing-in-the-background.md), backgrounds in Elements and backgrounds that move, without the model change that record expected for stage 4.

## Request

"אני רוצה להוסיף 2 אלמנט חדשים (לאלמנטים): "אנימציות" ו-"רקעים".
1. אני רוצה שגם הרקעים יוכלו להשתמש ב-"אנימציות" כמו בשקפים 1,2,4,6 במצגת "app\tests\import\מצגת רפרנס" (הכוונה לא לעיגולים המבהבים אלו לצורות הנעות בשקף)
2. ניתן יהיה להשתמש באנימציה גם ברכיב בפני עצמו (כולל הגדלה, הזזה, סיבוב וכו') והוא יכול לבוא כצורה (נקודה, קו, פוליגון, עיגול וכו') או לא."

Two pictures came with it, cut from the reference deck: a dashed orbit with dots around a hub, and small boxes riding a dashed path.

## Context

- The covers of the reference deck (`tests/import/מצגת רפרנס.html`, slides 1, 2, 4 and 6) draw their motion on a `<canvas>` with a script: `flow` (traces with lights running along them), `waves` (seven waves that swell in the middle), `converge` (particles that settle into three turning rings) and `orbit` (a sphere with three tilted orbits). Its other slides move with SVG: `animateMotion` along dashed paths, and CSS `@keyframes`.
- A deck holds no script ([ADR-071](ADR-071-bug-hunt-fixes.md)), and an HTML import brings a canvas in as a still picture. A picture may move, though. The cleaning of an `svg` element (`picture.ts` of the renderer) keeps its stylesheet with its `@keyframes`, and its SMIL (`animate`, `animateMotion`, `set`), unless an animation writes a link or a handler. The picture is drawn in a shadow root of its own on the stage, in the filmstrip, in a show and in an exported file. Nothing pauses it there.
- ADR-125 gave a background a drawing (`Background.elements`). It left two stages: a collection of backgrounds in Elements, and backgrounds that move. For the second it said that the model has no animation that plays in a loop outside the slide's timeline.
- The theme's colours reach into the shadow root as variables. A recoloured `svg` element (`colorOverrides`, SHP-06) is recoloured in its attributes only, not in its stylesheet.

## Decision

- **No change to the deck's schema.** An animation of Elements is an `svg` element whose markup moves by itself. Whatever turns, grows or flows does so in the picture's own stylesheet; whatever travels along a path or changes its shape does so with SMIL. As an element it moves, resizes, turns, mirrors and recolours like any other `svg` element, takes the slide's entrance animations, and is copied, grouped and undone the same way. A background of Elements is a `Background` whose drawing (`elements`, ADR-125) is one such element the size of the slide.
- **Colours.** The markup paints in six fixed colours, the reference deck's own (`INK` in `motionKit.ts`). Each one stands for a token: primary, secondary, accent, text, surface and bg. These colours are written as presentation attributes, never in the stylesheet. The element maps the ones it uses to their tokens in `colorOverrides` (`inkOverrides`). So the drawing follows the template and any later change of it, and row B lists its colours so they can be changed. The one exception is `GLINT`, a white highlight on the orbit's sphere, which is light in every theme.
- **The animations** (`motionPieces.ts`, 18), by how they move: along a path (orbit, boxes on a path, circuit trace, atom, dot on a line), turning (hexagons, rings, radar), growing and pulsing (ripples, a breathing shape, rising bars), lines and waves (waves, a drawn arrow, a flowing connector, a heartbeat), and particles and networks (network, gathering particles, rising bubbles). Some are one shape that moves: a dot, a line, a polygon, a circle. Others are made of many parts. The orbit and the boxes on a path are the two pieces in the pictures. The boxes on a path are the line alone, without the nodes at its ends, so it joins whatever the slide puts there. A piece that runs from one side to the other (`reads`: the arrow, the connector, the slider, the trace, the boxes, the bars, the heartbeat) is drawn left to right and mirrored in a deck that reads from the right, as a new line is. A click puts a piece in the middle of the slide, beside whatever already stands there, selected, in one undo step (`insertMotion`).
- **The backgrounds** (`motionBackgrounds.ts`, 11): the four motions of the reference deck, redrawn as pictures from the same formulas and constants (`flow`, `waves`, `converge`, `orbit`), four more that move (`radar`, `network`, `drift`, the reference deck's ambient blobs over its dot field, and `particles`), and three still ones (`dots`, `contours`, `arcs`). The blinking circles of the reference deck are left out, as asked. A drawing beside the text (`sided`) stands where the reference has it, on the left of a slide that reads from the right, and is mirrored (`flipH`) for a deck that reads from the left; ADR-125's mirror turns it again when the deck changes direction. A click gives the current slide, and every other slide selected in the filmstrip, the ground it shows (its fill and overlay; a photograph gives way to the theme's `bg`) with the drawing over it, replacing any drawing it had, in one undo step (`applyMotionBackground`). The Background tool goes back to the template's background, and its **Apply to all slides** spreads the background, drawing included.
- **What a picture without SMIL shows.** The PowerPoint export embeds the SVG of the picture next to its PNG, and PowerPoint draws SVG without SMIL. A shape that only its path places stands at the picture's origin, so every travelling shape is hidden (`opacity="0"`) until a `set` (or its own opacity animation) shows it. Without SMIL it is left out rather than drawn in the corner. What CSS animates rests in a state that reads as the drawing: the particles of `converge` gathered, the drawn arrow whole.
- **The panel.** Elements has two more collections, **Animations** (by group, with a search) and **Backgrounds** (moving, then still). The first screen's search finds both, by their names in the language of the interface, in Hebrew and in English, and by their group's name. Each tile is a small slide drawn by the renderer in the open deck's theme and direction: the piece, large, on the theme's ground, or the slide's ground with the background's drawing. A tile is still, held at 4 seconds into its motion, a moment chosen so that every piece shows what it is. It moves while the pointer or the keyboard is on it, and never when the system asks for less motion. A background's tile draws its picture through a filter that widens its lines by a few slide pixels and strengthens what is faint. A line of 2 pixels is a fifth of a pixel in a tile, and several of the backgrounds were the same empty tile. The slide is drawn as it is. A background's tile is marked pressed when the current slide shows it.
- **Strings** in all sixteen languages of the interface (`elements` namespace: `animations`, `backgrounds`, two collection names and two history labels).

## Consequences

- A deck that uses them holds markup: up to 7 KB an animation, and up to 33 KB a background on each slide that has it. The largest is the `waves` background: SMIL morphs each of its seven lines through ten shapes. The unit tests hold them under 16 KB and 48 KB.
- The motion lives inside the picture. The Animations panel does not list it, its speed cannot be set, and a show's black screen or a step does not hold it. It plays from the moment the slide is drawn. `converge` gathers once each time its slide is shown and then turns, as on the reference deck's cover.
- In PowerPoint the picture is still. Text, positions and colours stay as the slide drew them; what travelled along a path is not there.
- The agent sees the drawing when it reads a background. It does not write backgrounds with one (ADR-125, `AgentBackground`), and it has no tool for the animations of Elements.
- A deck's only slide, untouched, is spared the design check. With a background from Elements, as with any background, it is no longer that slide, and L07 asks for content on it.

## Verification

- Unit tests (`motion.test.ts`, 126): every piece and background is one picture in its own units, moves when it should, paints only in `INK` (and `GLINT`), with no colour in its stylesheet, hides what only a path places, and stays under its size. Each is named in Hebrew and English. The element lands in the middle in the theme's tokens and steps aside from what is there, mirrored by `reads`. A background keeps the ground, puts a photograph aside, and goes on several slides through a validating `CommandBus` in one undo step, each with a drawing of its own, mirrored by `sided`. Undo gives back the slides' backgrounds.
- In Edge (`motion.browser.test.tsx`, 30): through the real renderer and its cleaning, every animation keeps its SMIL and its `@keyframes`, has CSS animations running, and is drawn differently at 1 and at 2.3 seconds. The orbit paints in the theme's text and accent colours, and its travelling dots are shown. Every background is drawn the size of the slide, and moves if and only if it should.
- End to end in Edge (`elements-motion.spec.ts`, 4, and the collections test of `elements-panel.spec.ts`), against the mocked backend: the two collections on the first screen. An animation lands in the middle, selected, in one undo step, and moves on the stage. The arrow is mirrored by the deck's direction. A tile is still, moves under the pointer and stops again. A background goes behind the slide in one step, its tile is pressed, its drawing moves on the stage, another takes its place, and undo gives back the slide's ground. The first screen's search finds both kinds in Hebrew and in English.
- Visual review. Every animation was rendered at full slide size at three moments, and every background behind a cover title, each in a light and a dark built-in template, the backgrounds in Hebrew and in English. Screenshots of the panel were taken in both languages. The review led to these changes: the network laid on a jittered grid, the particles made soft, the contours smoothed, the sphere lit with a white glint that works on a dark template, the breathing shape drawn in one hue (two hues turned muddy), the bars grown by their height (scaling squashed their corners), still moments chosen for three pieces, a blob kept inside its picture, and the background tiles strengthened.
- Under load, with three workers, the background test ran past Playwright's 30 seconds in three of six repeats (alone it takes 14), and one run reported page errors whose text was not kept; with 90 seconds five of six passed, and the sixth did not open the app at all. Six other sessions were working on the same machine at the time.
- Type check of the desktop app (no error in the files of this record; the working tree has errors from a `disabled` field that another change is adding), ESLint and Prettier on the changed files, and `translations.test.ts`. `designs.test.ts` fails as before ("296 first designs" finds 286).

## Open for the user

- Whether a background from Elements should also be offered as a template's background variant, and whether the agent should be able to choose one.
- Whether the speed of an animation should be adjustable (it would be a field on the element, read by the renderer as a playback rate), or whether its motion should follow the show (start with an entrance, hold on a black screen). The speed was answered by [ADR-128](ADR-128-motion-that-repeats-and-animated-shapes.md): row B's Motion tool draws the picture again at another speed.
- The filmstrip's thumbnail of a slide with a moving background moves too, as the renderer draws it. Whether a thumbnail should be still (`mode="thumbnail"` in the renderer) is open; it was not measured on a long deck.
- The pieces were reviewed in the app's mocked browser setup and in Edge, not in the packaged app's window, and not in an exported PowerPoint file opened in PowerPoint.
